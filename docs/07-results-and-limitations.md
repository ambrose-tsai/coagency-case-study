# 07 · Results & Limitations

## What I can say

> Colleagues using CoAgency internally said that **creating a new opportunity went from about 30 minutes to about 5 minutes.**

Where the time goes:

- Notes are pasted or pulled in once, and the visit log and deal fields are drafted for the rep.
- The next step and the follow-up email are drafted from the same context.
- The rep reviews instead of typing everything from scratch.

Qualitative feedback:

- Reps were more willing to log activities when the job was "check and confirm" instead of "fill in the form."
- Managers found the risk alerts and Deal Coach gaps useful as a coaching agenda.

## What I'm not claiming

| Not claimed | Why |
|---|---|
| A publicly launched SaaS product | CoAgency has been used internally only |
| Paying customers or revenue | There are none |
| ROI multiples | No controlled measurement was done |
| Replacement of all sales tools | It covers the core sales loop, not the whole GTM stack |
| Fully automated CRM | Autopilot is designed but off; people approve external actions |

## Known limitations

- The time savings come from user feedback, not instrumented before/after data.
- Only a small internal group has used it, so there's no multi-company validation yet.
- Screen-reader testing is incomplete (some checks were postponed to a machine with NVDA).
- Only Gmail and Google Calendar are supported so far.

## What I'd do next

1. Add instrumentation to measure time-to-opportunity and draft acceptance rates.
2. Run a pilot with an external sales team.
3. Turn on Autopilot only for low-risk internal actions (for example, reminders), never for customer email first.
4. Extend the same draft-and-approve pattern to customer-success handoff.
